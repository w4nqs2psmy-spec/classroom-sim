AURORA & SILENCE
The Kittila Village Tourism Co-operative - a case study

1. Background

The village of Alakittila (pop. 214) in Finnish Lapland formed a tourism
co-operative in 2019. Eleven households are members. The co-op owns six
guest cabins, a small smoke sauna by the lake, and a shared kitchen
building, and it part-owns a husky farm with 28 dogs together with a
neighbouring village. Total tourism revenue last year was 410,000 euros,
of which 96 percent was earned between December and March.

2. The occupancy problem

Cabin occupancy by season (last full year):

  December - March:   92 percent
  April - May:        14 percent
  June - August:      31 percent
  September - November: 22 percent

The co-op chair, Marja-Leena K., summarised the board's view at the
spring meeting: "We don't need more February. February is full. We need
May. We need a reason for anyone to be here in May."

3. Stakeholder voices

The husky farm partner, Pekka R., is under growing pressure: "Every
winter there are more articles about sled-dog welfare. We do things
properly, but nobody visits a kennel to check. We are one bad article
away from closing, and the whole village's winter product goes with us."

A young returnee, Sanna V. (28), moved back from Rovaniemi to work the
winter season: "There is work here from December to March, and then
there is nothing. If the co-op cannot give me a summer, I will have to
leave again, and I am not the only one."

A member household that rents out two of the six cabins worries about
scale: "We joined so that visitors would come to us, not so that we
would become Levi. Whatever we build next must stay small enough that
it is still our village."

4. Constraints

The co-operative's statute requires that at least 60 percent of all
tourism revenue remains within the village (wages, purchases, rents).
Any new product must respect this rule. The board has allocated up to
40,000 euros for developing ONE new product for the coming year.

The board has deliberately not decided what that product should be.
That decision is the assignment.
