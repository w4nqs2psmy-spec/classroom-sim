TWO BRIEFINGS ON THE SAFESIGHT PROPOSAL
Read both pages before the group discussion.

PAGE ONE - Vendor briefing (SafeSight Ltd.)

SafeSight is an AI camera system that identifies risky behaviour in
school environments in real time - altercations, bullying situations,
unauthorised area access - and alerts staff before incidents escalate.

Key figures from our deployments:
  - 92 percent detection accuracy across all monitored behaviours
  - A reviewed alert takes staff under 30 seconds on average
  - Reference school (11 months): reported incidents down 34 percent

The system flags behaviour, not identity: no face recognition database
is built, and video is retained for 72 hours only. Staff always make
the final decision; SafeSight only recommends. In our reference
deployments, staff describe the dashboard as "a colleague who never
blinks."

SafeSight offers your institution a full deployment covering shared
spaces (corridors, cafeteria, yard) with staff training included.

PAGE TWO - Statement (Digital Rights Watch)

The vendor's own headline number deserves a second look. 92 percent
accuracy means 8 percent of flags are wrong. In a school of roughly
1,100 students, at the alert volumes SafeSight itself reports, that is
approximately 90 students every week flagged for behaviour that was
not risky at all. The vendor sheet does not say who has those 90
conversations, how a flagged student is approached, or what record
remains.

Three further points the vendor sheet omits:

1. The accuracy figure is self-reported and has not been independently
   audited. No deployment of this system has published its false-flag
   statistics.

2. Audits of comparable systems consistently show that "risky
   behaviour" classifiers flag some students more than others - louder
   students, larger students, students in hoodies. The error rate is
   not distributed evenly, and the students already under the most
   scrutiny absorb most of it.

3. Chilling effects are documented: students who know an algorithm is
   watching change how they gather, speak and move - whether or not
   they are ever flagged.

A decision to adopt, reject or pilot should not be made from the
vendor's page alone. Both pages of this brief describe the same system.
